### 📍 1. Use LinkedIn’s Official Appeal Form (No Login Needed)

➡️ Go to:
[https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/TS-F-APPEAL](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/TS-F-APPEAL)
This is LinkedIn’s restricted account appeal form specifically for situations where your account was limited or disabled.

You may need to:
• Include your email address associated with the account
• Provide your full name
• Explain that you submitted your government ID but the restriction hasn’t been lifted
• Attach any supporting information

This is usually the *primary* way to reach LinkedIn Support for an account restriction.

---

### 📍 2. Submit an Account Access Support Case

➡️ Try submitting this support request if the appeal form doesn’t work:
[https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/sdsupport](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/sdsupport)
This opens a general support case where you can explain the situation and ask for help reviewing your ID verification.

---

### 📍 3. Try Other LinkedIn Support URLs (Sometimes Necessary)

If the above two don’t accept your message, other LinkedIn support paths that users sometimes use include:
• Restricted removal request: [https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/TS-EPR](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/TS-EPR)
• Another appeal option: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/TS-RHA
• Contact Data Protection Officer: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/TSO-DPO
(Note: All of these are *official LinkedIn Help Center paths*, not email inboxes.)

---

### 📍 4. Contact LinkedIn on Social Media

LinkedIn doesn’t respond by phone, but you can message them on:
• X (Twitter) — [https://x.com/LinkedInHelp](https://x.com/LinkedInHelp)
• Facebook — [https://www.facebook.com/LinkedIn/](https://www.facebook.com/LinkedIn/)

Sometimes support teams respond to direct messages on these platforms if support tickets aren’t moving.

---

### 📌 Tips for Your Appeal

When you fill the appeal form or support ticket, keep these points:
✔️ Be polite and concise
✔️ Mention that you’ve already *submitted ID verification* (and when)
✔️ Explain that the system says *you already submitted a request* but you never saw confirmation or resolution
✔️ Ask clearly: *“Can you please review my verification and restore access?”*

hey kabar! thanks for raising this clearly.

In general from both a scoring and integrity point of view, sending clearly broken outputs is the worst option. If a miner has collapsed and is returning zeros or otherwise invalid responses, that usually hurts more than a clean temporary outage, because the miner is still being evaluated but with obviously degraded behavior.

So as a general rule:

c) is the worst case: continuing to respond with broken outputs
b) is the safest fallback if the deployed model is no longer functioning correctly
a) can be acceptable only if that fallback is already properly declared, traceable, and consistent with the published repo/manifest/code reference

The key point for us is integrity and reproducibility. If a uid temporarily points to a different undeclared model during the daily session, that creates a consistency problem. On the other hand, if a fallback is genuinely part of the published and verifiable deployment design, that is a different situation.

So the practical guidance would be:

if the active model is broken, do not keep serving obviously invalid outputs
if you have a fallback, it should be part of the same declared, reproducible setup
